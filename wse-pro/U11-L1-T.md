People who don't speak the same language can easily misunderstand each other, and the potential for misunderstanding is even greater when people come from different cultures with very different value systems, as with Asian and Western cultures. One way of bridging the gap is by studying local customs even before you arrive. Being aware of other people' s culture and customs is more than just being polite-it promotes trust and understanding. This is especially true when Westerns and Asians do business.
For those who are doing business in a different culture, some knowledge of etiquette is helpful, of course. For example, in Asian countries, people often greet each other with a bow or a slight nod of the head. Sometimes a handshake is acceptable, but it's best to wait for the host or Asian colleague to hold out a hand. One should never pat a child on the top of the head. In fact, most forms of physical contact, such as patting someone on the back, should be avoided until visitors feel confident that they understand what is or isn't acceptable.
Another example is the way business cards are exchanged in Asian cultures. When you receive a business card, you should take it with both hands and avoid folding or writing on it so that you don' t insult the other person. When you are seated, showing the soles of your shoes or pointing your shoe towards anyone is considered rude in many Asian cultures because the bottoms of shoes are considered unclean.
Similarly, Westerners may feel uncomfortable when someone they have just met asks questions about their job, salary, marital status, and age. Such questions are normal in many Asian cultures, whereas many Westerners would consider them far too personal.
Simple rules of politeness may not be enough, however. In China, the concept of guanxi" is extremely important in doing business. It is the idea of having a network of people you know and trust; these people do favors for each other. Though guanxi is a Chinese word, the idea exists in many cultures, especially in East Asia. In a guanxi relationship, people have something in common and feel an obligation to help one another. The relationship usually takes time to establish, so patience is important. Westerners who are too impatient to take the time needed find themselves losing business deals in the end.
Asians like to establish a solid relationship with business partners, in contrast with some Westerners, who may consider the connection only short-term. Westerners are more likely than Asians to be direct and to express disagreement openly, whereas Asians tend to be more indirect and reluctant to appear confrontational. The different approaches can lead to confusion and sometimes resentment. Asians may find it difficult to say no, and rather than risk disappointing a colleague or customer, might say "yes" or "maybe", while all the time indicating "no" through body language.. In some cultures, such as Japan, people might say "That would be difficult," when they really mean "No, it can't be done" The phrase is often misunderstood by those from other cultures.
One of the greast challenges in doing business in a different culture is to avoid applying one's own values to that culture.
